# Craft rules

The rules behind `toolkit/vg_lib/prims.py`. Each rule exists once in code and once here. Source tags: **[IBM]** IBM Design Language, technical diagrams and line-style illustration; **[Material]** Google Material icon guidance; **[WCAG]** WCAG 2.1 contrast; **[draft]** engineering drafting standards; **[mine]** judgement, adjust against the eye and record the change here.

## Principles

- **Rank.** Stroke weight and colour say what matters: connectors outweigh outlines, outlines outweigh guides. One accent colour marks the one thing the figure argues. **[IBM]** caps weights at four per figure.
- **Rhythm.** Repeated parts are identical in size and evenly spaced, on a grid. Positions snap to the variant grid (4 wide, 2 medium, 1 narrow).
- **Shared edges.** Where two shapes join, the joint is square and the free corner carries the radius. A tab on a frame shares the frame's top-left radius; its other joint corners stay square. **[Material]** keeps interior corners square. Bottom-left of a tabbed frame is square: the left edge reads as one line from tab to base. **[mine]**
- **Floor.** Text renders at 11px or more at the smallest width its variant serves. A variant that cannot reach 11px is not drawn; the generator raises and the author shortens the label or changes the form. One exception, set by the founder 2026-10-21: sequence participant names stay on one line. When they do not fit at the variant size, all of them step down together to `fit_min` (10px), then the glyphs squeeze by up to `fit_squeeze` (0.89). Those texts carry `data-fit` and are exempt from the floor gate; nothing else is.
- **Second cue.** Colour never carries meaning alone: accent also changes weight, dash, fill or label weight. **[WCAG]** 1.4.1.
- **Restraint.** No shadows, gradients or decoration. Every mark earns its place by carrying the claim.

## Scales (tokens, `toolkit/tokens/default.json`)

| Scale | Values | Use |
|---|---|---|
| stroke | guide 1 · outline 1.5 · connector 2 · emphasis 3 | lifelines and dividers · node and frame outlines · arrows · added or accented nodes |
| radius | s 4 · m 8 | free corner of tabs and chips · nodes and frames. A node takes the radius nearest a sixth of its short side, so small boxes get 4 and large boxes 8. **[IBM]** 8px nodes |
| type | 11 · 12 · 14 · 16 · 20 | label sizes per variant (`node_font`, `edge_font`, `tab_font`, `title_font`) |
| grid | 4 wide · 2 medium · 1 narrow | box position and size |
| arrow head | length 6 × connector stroke (12px), half-width 0.36 × length (≈8.6px wide) | filled, same colour as its line, tip on the target edge. **[draft]** gives long narrow heads, UI heads run squat; this sits between. **[mine]** |

## Element rules

- **Node.** Equal size within a group. Text centred on cap height (baseline at centre + 0.345em). Label wraps to two lines only when one line cannot fit; all nodes in the figure keep one height.
- **Concurrency and repetition.** Two messages at one height that share a source (fan-out) or destination (fan-in) are a `row`: the picture says "at the same time". The short one labels above its run, the long one below, on the part beyond the short one; both dashed lines start at the shared end so their dashes coincide. A loop carries `repeat`: an accent bracket right of the shared participant, from the tip of the last message to the tip of the first, with no head of its own and 8px clear of the frame edge; a second head faced the first one and read as a collision. Lanes (below) show alternatives in sequence; rows show simultaneity.
- **Frame with tab.** A frame holding parallel work uses `lanes`: each lane keeps its own time order, a dashed rule (outline weight, muted) separates lanes, as in UML `par`. Frame outline: muted dashed (neutral) or accent solid (the loop under argument). Tab: surface fill (neutral) or accent fill with dark text. Frame aligns to the outer edges of the participants it spans and clears the participant row by 16px.
- **Fork and join (bus).** A fork, one source with several targets, leaves the middle of the box in one trunk, runs along a bar and drops straight into the middle of each target; a join mirrors it and ends in one arrowhead into the middle of the target. Every drop is straight, so no jog reads as a kink (a critic asked for this in every round on the feed image, 2026-10-22). Labels sit on the run into each target (fork) or out of each source (join), on the outer side of a vertical run, above a horizontal one. Where a join has several sources the head belongs to one edge (the one in line with the target, else the accent one) and the others end on the bar. The bus needs a layer gap of the label run, the bar and a last run of two heads, so the gap grows when a fork or join is present; the build refuses a fork and a join between the same two layers (add a node) and a last run shorter than two heads. Single edges and edges that are both in a fork and in a join keep their own ports and a bend two heads before the target. **[mine]**
- **Digits.** The figure font (Georgia) has old-style figures: 1 and 2 sit on the x-height, 3, 4, 5, 7 and 9 drop below the baseline, so a row of numbers looks vertically uneven (found in the step badges, founder 2026-10-22). Every digit run is therefore set in the number font of the tokens (`font.value`, lining figures), in big values, badges and inside ordinary text alike; the build measures them with that font.
- **Text case.** Sentence case everywhere: the first letter of every text block is a capital (nodes, participants, edge labels, frame tabs, panel titles, the key), the other words are lower case except proper names and acronyms (API, Redis, PostgreSQL). Not Title Case, and not lower case for one kind of text and capitalised for another. The build refuses a block that starts with a lower-case letter. Founder decision 2026-10-22.
- **Back edge** (flow, `"back": true`). A return to an earlier box, for a retry or a feedback loop. It runs outside the figure in the same orthogonal style, down the right side in a top-down flow and along the bottom in a left-to-right one, dashed by default so it reads as a return, and enters the earlier box from outside with a last run of two heads. The first lane is two heads and a margin from the boxes, further lanes 14px apart, and the label sits beside the lane (below it in a left-to-right flow). The build refuses a back edge whose route would cross another box. Curved arrows are not part of the toolkit: they would need a second line language and its own gates, and the orthogonal return does the same job.
- **Edge.** Orthogonal. Return messages dashed; an asynchronous edge (`async`: the sender does not wait) ends in an open chevron head, a synchronous one in a filled head **[draft]** UML convention, and a compare key names the open head when it is used; dash means "return" or "removed" and nothing else. In a flow every edge owns its ports: points on the box side spread over two thirds of its length, neighbours at least three head widths apart (and a label height + 8 apart when labels sit above the runs), so fan-out and fan-in mirror each other and no two heads share a point. Bends of edges that approach one target from the same side step 8px apart, the farthest source lowest; edges from opposite sides bend at one height. The bend sits two heads before the target, so the head stands on a straight run with a shaft as long as the head. Gaps between layers are at least four head lengths; a gap that carries a label is label width + 44px. The label rides the first run: above a horizontal run, beside a vertical one, always at the same offset. In a top-down flow it sits on the outer side of the line (left for the left-most edge of a fork or join, right for the others), so a fork and its join mirror each other. A label sits on the run that belongs to that edge alone and carries a background-coloured halo so lines behind it break. An edge may span layers only as a straight line; back edges are not supported.
- **Icon.** Line icons only, from the vendored Lucide set (24-unit drawing, ISC), so every icon shares one stroke and corner style. Size comes from the icon scale (16, 20, 24, 32, 48; each variant names its default), stroke from the stroke scale (outline, 1.5), colour from the node role (ink, accent, or muted when dimmed). An icon sits above its label as one block centred in the box; a figure gives every node an icon or none, so boxes keep one size. A product logo (`brand:`) is one filled glyph in a token colour, never the brand colour, so the palette stays closed. One figure uses one kind: line icons for generic parts, or logos when every node is a named product; mixing them gives two visual families and the build refuses it (a critic round, 2026-10-22). Icons never replace the label. **[IBM]** line-style illustration, one weight. **[mine]**
- **Sequence participants and labels.** Participants take an icon like flow nodes (every participant or none). A message label sits 10px above its arrow, so a descender (p, g) keeps clear of the line. When a lifeline would cut through the label, the label moves to the longest clear stretch of the arrow between lifelines, the rightmost on a tie, so neighbouring labels share a column. A label that fits no clear stretch keeps its centre and breaks the lifeline with an 8px background halo. A lifeline also breaks for 10px where an arrow crosses it, so dashes never run into a dashed return arrow. A critic called the off-centre labels on long arrows a DEFECT in the feed image (2026-10-22); the founder rule stands, so this is an open finding. **[mine]**, founder 2026-10-22.
- **Guide.** Lifelines and dividers use the guide stroke and the hair colour. They are exempt from graphic contrast because nothing depends on seeing them.
- **Title, key and panels.** A comparison names each panel (title font, bold). The key is generated from the encodings the figure uses, never written by hand, so it cannot promise a mark that is absent. It wraps to one entry per line when it does not fit the width. Panels share one layout: a box with the same id sits at the same place in every panel. A solid hairline splits panels; dashes stay reserved. Flows and comparisons keep a frame pad (12 narrow, 16 medium, 24 wide) around the content; only the sequence diagram runs tighter, because its labels need the width.

## Infographic forms (`cards`, `steps`, `hub`, `cycle`, `matrix`, `funnel`, `swimlanes`, `layers`)

Specs are in the module docstrings (`vg_lib/cards.py`, `steps.py`, `hub.py`, `cycle.py`, `matrix.py`, `funnel.py`, `swimlanes.py`, `layers.py`) and one example each in `examples/`. All four build at the same variants and the feed image as a flow, and run through the same gates.

- **Cards** (also statistics). Equal cards in a grid: icon (every card or none), a big value in the figure font for numbers (lining digits; Georgia has old-style ones, where 0 looks like o), a bold title, a muted text. Columns are the most that keep the text of each card at least `cards.min_text` wide with the last row at least half full; an incomplete last row stretches over the full width instead of leaving a gap, and a set of three or fewer stacks in a single column in the feed image. Titles in one row reserve the same number of lines so the captions start level. In a fixed frame the cards grow, by at most half, to fill it, with their content centred. One accent card at most carries the point, and it is the card that proves the claim. Numbers are set bigger in the feed image (`stat_font` 40) so they dominate.
- **Steps.** Numbered badges (accent fill, dark number). A timeline in one row when each column is at least `steps.min_column` wide, otherwise a list top-down with a line joining the badges. The title sits level with the badge.
- **Hub.** One accent node in the middle, drawn a quarter larger than the others. Two to four spokes sit in a cross (left and right, then up and down) joined by straight lines, which is what makes it read as a hub; when three columns do not fit the width, and for five or six spokes, the spokes sit in a row above and a row below on the same bus as a flow fork. `"arrows": "out"` puts a head on each spoke; the default is plain lines.
- **Cycle.** Four, five, six or eight steps clockwise from the top left, a straight arrow to the next and the last back to the first. The arrow that closes the loop is the point of a loop: it is drawn in the accent colour and heavier, and `return_label` names it beside the arrow. Five steps put three boxes on top and two below with the middle column empty. A figure with another count is refused.
- **Matrix.** Four cells, two arrows pointing to "more" (the y arrow left, the x arrow under the grid), each named "More ..." at its head, so "low" is the other end without a word. In a fixed frame the cells grow, by at most half, to fill it. The accent cell is the one the claim recommends.
- **Funnel.** Stacked centred bars stepping down, a name left and a value right in each; a bar too narrow for its value carries it just outside. The loss between two stages is a `drop` label in the accent between the bars: the funnel is a drawing, so the values and the drop carry the data, not the bar widths. Values are ink, the accent stage's value is the accent.
- **Swimlanes.** Lanes are columns and time runs down, so a phone holds three lanes. Steps are in time order, one row each; consecutive steps are joined by an arrow, straight in the same lane, otherwise down to a bar, across and down. Lane boxes leave a gap between them so no dashed outline is drawn twice; an edge between steps that are not consecutive is not supported.
- **Layers.** A stack read bottom-up: groups are tinted containers (`warm`, `cool` tone tokens: a tinted fill and a same-hue 1.5px outline, text stays ink on the tint), layers inside are neutral `surface` bars or a grid of equal chips. Bars share one height; name and caption sit on one line with the captions in one aligned column when every bar fits that way, else the caption goes under the name. Independent modules form one grid on one level, in the most even columns (nine make three by three). A group's logo sits at the right end of its header in the tone colour, so labels align across groups (the Oracle logo is hand-added in `brands-extra.json`, supplied by the founder 2026-10-22, drawn in the tone colour, not Oracle red). A short labelled arrow ("Runs on") joins each group to the one below, bars may carry step numbers and, optionally, the base bar a heavier tone outline (spec key `base`; the founder had it removed from the platform figure on 2026-10-22: it reads as a selected row without a legend, the numbers and the arrow carry the order): three critics in a row read the stack as a list until these cues were added. Chips of one layer must all be one line or all two (the build refuses a mix). Two tones separate two kinds of layer; a third is not supported. No feed image (`no_social`: the stack is tall). Founder 2026-10-22: platform layers in the repo order, OCI warm, Kubernetes cool with its logo.
- **Common.** A wrapped line is a continuation: only the first line of a block needs the capital. Sizes, gaps and fonts come from the variant tokens, never from the spec.

## One palette for charts and diagrams

`dataviz` keeps the palette in a markdown file with two tables: the categorical hues (`| slot | hue | light | dark |`) and "Chart chrome & ink" (`| role | light | dark |`). `vg.py tokens` turns it into a token override: Page plane to `bg`, Chart surface to `surface`, Primary ink to `ink`, Secondary ink to `muted`, Baseline to `hair`, categorical slot 1 to `accent`; `--font sans` also sets the system sans the method uses. The mapping is fixed in `vg_lib/tokens.py` (`ROLES`); a file without those rows is refused with the missing names. Checked on the reference palette in both modes: the light accent (#2a78d6) gives 4.2:1 as text on its surface, under the 4.5:1 text gate, so the command warns and `--accent` takes a darker step. A wider font (the sans) can make a label that fitted in the serif too long for a narrow lane; the build then refuses it rather than squeezing it. Only the first categorical slot is used by the figures, because a diagram marks one thing; further slots stay for charts.

## Variants and breakpoints

Each figure builds at narrow (343), mid (about 456), medium (about 600) and wide (about 760, the page column cap) widths. Each variant is valid from `viewBox width × 11 ÷ smallest font` pixels of image width upward; `vg.py` prints that number and writes the `<picture>` snippet with the breakpoints shifted by `page_gutter` (32px of page margin by default; set the real gutter in the project token override, a site with 64px of margin from about 480px of viewport up gets breakpoints 520, 662 and 824), because the media query sees the viewport and the image sees the column. A variant is never shown larger than about 1.35 times its drawn size, so text stays between 11 and about 15px and the jump at each switch is at most a quarter. Below 343px the figure is unsupported. Message pitch (`row`) is 34 narrow, 38 mid, 44 medium, 46 wide: a phone figure is dense, not airy. A flow switches itself from left-to-right to top-down when left-to-right does not fit the variant width.

## Feed image (`social`, `vg_lib/social.py`)

Every build also writes `NAME-social.png`, the image for a LinkedIn post: 1080px wide, ratio between 1:1 and 4:5 (height 1080 to 1350). LinkedIn does not take SVG, so this one is a raster export of the same spec.

- **Canvas.** The figure is drawn 360 units wide, the width of the narrowest phone feed, and exported at 3x. Text is checked at 360px, so the 11px floor holds on the smallest phone; the floor for this image is 12px (`social.min_font`).
- **Fill the frame.** The build tries type steps from large to small (`social.tiers`, then the base step) and keeps the first whose figure fits 4:5, so the figure is as large as the frame allows. A figure at least 75% of the way to 4:5 is centred in a 4:5 frame; a shorter one is centred in a square. A figure taller than 4:5 at the base step is refused: cut nodes or labels, or split it in two images.
- **Flow runs top-down** (portrait). A before/after figure in this image only works when both panels fit left-to-right in 360 units; most do not, so give each panel its own image or set `"no_social": true` in the spec.
- **Sequence** works up to four participants with short names; five or more fail the label-fit check. `agent-flow` and `queue-compare` set `"no_social": true` for these reasons.
- **Gates.** All figure gates at 360px, plus ratio, exact pixel size, PNG at most 5 MB, and no text within 16px of the canvas edge (`text_margin`; a figure that breaks it drops to the next smaller type step). Edges outside a fork or join keep wider ports than on the site (`port_gap` 80).
- **Source of the numbers.** Third-party guides agree that LinkedIn shows a feed image whole up to 4:5 (1080 x 1350 is the usual portrait size), accepts PNG and JPEG up to 5 MB, and that portrait images take more phone screen. LinkedIn's own help page was not reachable when these were checked, so re-check against it before relying on an exact limit. The reach claim for portrait images is in `linkedin-presence/references/evidence.md`.

## Gates (`vg_lib/check.py`)

`vg.py check FILE.svg` runs the subset that needs no toolkit marks on any SVG (palette, stroke, radius and type scales, contrast, text size at the display width, text overlap, canvas edge) and says it is partial.

Token gates (SVG text): every text block starts with a capital letter · colours are tokens · stroke widths are in the scale, four distinct at most · radii are in the scale · font sizes are in the type scale · text contrast 4.5:1 against its own background · edge, frame and group strokes 3:1 against the canvas · accent has a second cue.
Geometry gates (Chromium, `getBBox`): a group or lane label at least half its font size below the top of its box (found in the swimlane lane names, founder 2026-10-22) · text ≥ 11px at the variant's smallest width · text inside its shape · no text overlap · text at least 3px from foreign nodes and tabs · every arrow at least three heads long, its last run at least two heads · equal size and equal gaps in each group · grid snap · arrowhead ≥ 5 strokes long and ≥ 4 strokes wide · frames inside the canvas and clear of nodes · edges clear of foreign nodes · labels off foreign edges · icon size on the icon scale, stroke in the stroke scale, 3:1 contrast, inside its box, at least 14px on screen, 3px from text, equal size in a group · no dead strip (space under the content at most twice the space above, plus 8px).

Gates catch what is measurable. They do not catch ugly: run the critic round (`critic.md`). Blind test, 2026-10-21: with arrowheads shrunk and every frame corner rounded, a critic who had not seen the code named the corner collision and the small heads unprompted; on the clean build it raised no corner or arrowhead finding.
